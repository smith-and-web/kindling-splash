We make kindling, a free writing app that imports Plottr files, so we have a stake in how you read this. We have tried to write the review we would want if we were the ones deciding: what Plottr does well, what it costs, and where it stops.

**How much is Plottr?** The standard plan is $60 a year or $150 once, for the Mac and Windows app. Plottr Pro, which adds the browser app and cloud sync, is $9.99 a month, $99 a year or $599 once. Prices were checked on Plottr's own pages on 11 November 2026, in USD before tax.

## The short verdict

Plottr is the best dedicated visual outliner for novelists, and its standard plan is fairly priced: $150 once for the Mac and Windows app, on as many household computers as you like. It is not a writing app. You plan in Plottr and write somewhere else, and whether that suits you decides whether Plottr is worth it.

Buy it if you plan in detail, think in timelines or run a series. If you want one tool to outline and draft in, use the 30-day trial to find out whether the handoff to a second app bothers you before you pay.

## What Plottr is

[Plottr](https://plottr.com/features/) is a desktop app for Windows and macOS for planning fiction. Its centre is a timeline of scene cards laid out across plotlines, so you can see every thread of the book at once. Around it sit a story bible of characters, places and notes, and an outline that Plottr builds from the timeline for you.

## What Plottr does well

- **The timeline.** Drag scene cards between plotlines, colour-code subplots, and see the shape of the whole book on one screen. It is the most direct way we know to plan a novel visually.
- **Templates.** More than 30 plot and scene templates, from three-act structure to named beat sheets, give you a starting shape instead of an empty grid.
- **Series planning.** A series holds any number of books, with a view across all of them. For a long series, this alone can justify the price.
- **Fair terms on the standard plan.** Files stay on your computer, the app works offline, it has no AI, and one purchase covers every device in your household. If a yearly plan lapses, the app keeps working; it just stops updating.
- **A way out.** Plottr [exports to Word and Scrivener](https://docs.plottr.com/article/66-exporting), so the outline can follow you into a writing app.

## Where Plottr stops

- **There is no writing area.** Scene cards hold notes, not a manuscript. [Reedsy's review](https://reedsy.com/blog/guide/book-writing-software/plottr-review/) says the same: Plottr "doesn't have a writing area". When the outline is done, you export it and draft somewhere else, and the outline becomes a reference in another window.
- **Browser, tablet and sync live in Pro, at a different price.** Pro keeps projects in the cloud, so if a Pro plan lapses you cannot open them until you renew. More on [what Pro adds](#plottr-pro-what-it-adds-and-who-needs-it) below.
- **No phone app at the moment.** Plottr [removed its mobile app from the app stores](https://docs.plottr.com/article/117-mobile-new-mobile-app-incoming-please-read) because it "did not meet our high vision for Plottr mobile", and is rebuilding it with no date given. On a tablet, you use Pro's browser app.
- **Linux is unofficial.** A [beta AppImage and deb exist](https://docs.plottr.com/article/31-can-i-install-plottr-for-linux), but Plottr calls them unsupported beta software that may be discontinued at any time. Automatic updates do not work there.
- **Co-writing costs per person.** Each co-author needs their own Pro subscription.
- **No refunds.** Plottr offers up to five hours of outlining coaching instead, if the app does not help you finish an outline.

## Plottr Pro: what it adds and who needs it

Pro is the standard app plus four things, all built on Plottr's cloud:

- **The browser app.** Plottr in a web browser, which is how it runs on an iPad, an Android tablet or a Chromebook.
- **Cloud sync and backups.** Projects live in Plottr's cloud and stay in step across devices. On the standard plan, syncing is manual, through a storage service of your choice.
- **Real-time collaboration.** Two people can work in one project at once, if both pay for Pro.
- **Idea Threads sync.** Notes captured in Plottr's separate Idea Threads app flow into your projects.

**Pro is worth it** if you plan on a tablet or Chromebook, or co-write a book in real time. **It is not** if you plan at one desk, or between two computers you can keep in step with a shared folder. Remember the trade: Pro projects live in the cloud, so a lapsed Pro plan locks you out of them until you renew. The standard plan keeps your files on your own computer.

## Is Plottr worth it?

For a writer who outlines in detail, yes: the $150 lifetime plan is a fair price for the best visual outliner available. For a writer who wants to outline and draft in one place, Plottr covers only the first half, so budget for a writing app too.

**Plottr is a good buy if you:**

- plan scene by scene before you draft, and like to see the whole book at once;
- write a series and want plot threads tracked across books;
- already have a writing app you like, such as Scrivener or Word.

**Look elsewhere if you:**

- want to outline and draft in the same app;
- work mainly on a phone, or on Linux;
- discover the story as you write, and only sketch a few beats ahead.

### Which plan to buy

- **Most writers: the standard lifetime plan, $150.** It costs less than three years of the $60 yearly plan.
- **Pro, only for the browser app or co-writing.** Yearly Pro at $99 stays cheaper than lifetime Pro for six years.
- **Pro + Community is for the courses.** The extra $30 a year over Pro buys a private community and Plottr's course library, not more software.

## Does Plottr use AI?

No. The app has none, and the standard plan lists "No AI" among its features. The same team sells [Storysnap](https://storysnap.ai/), a separate paid service that uses AI to turn a finished manuscript into a Plottr outline: $50 per book, or $45 a book in bulk. It is optional and sits outside the app.

## Moving a Plottr outline into a writing app

Plottr's own exports go to Word and to Scrivener, from the toolbar on the timeline or outline view. Both carry the outline across as text you then write beneath.

kindling takes a different route: it opens the `.pltr` file itself, so there is no export step. What comes across:

- **Beats become chapters,** and **scene cards become scenes,** with their titles and descriptions. The description becomes the beat you write into.
- **Characters and places** keep their descriptions and custom attributes, such as role.
- **Who appears where:** the characters and places linked to each scene card stay linked, and appear in the References panel while you draft that scene.
- **Series name, premise, genre and theme** carry over as project details.

What does not, yet: bold and italic in card text become plain text, and Plottr tags are read but not shown in kindling. The import needs a Plottr file from 2023 or later. The full list is in the [Plottr import guide](/docs/importing-projects/#plottr-pltr).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Plottr a one-time purchase?

It can be. Every plan has a lifetime option: $150 for Plottr, $599 for Pro and $649 for Pro + Community. Plottr also sells yearly plans, and Pro plans monthly.

### Does Plottr have a free version?

No. There is a 30-day free trial, then a paid plan. For free options, including ones that also draft, see [Plottr vs Scrivener and the free alternatives](/plottr-vs-scrivener/).

### Does Plottr work on Linux, iPad or Android?

Only partly. The Linux build is an unsupported beta. On iPad, Android and Chromebooks, Plottr runs through the Pro plan's browser app; there is no native mobile app at the moment.

### Can I move my Plottr outline into a writing app?

Yes. Plottr exports to Word and Scrivener, and kindling opens `.pltr` files directly, keeping scenes, characters and places linked. Follow the guide to [turning an outline into a first draft](/blog/how-to-turn-your-novel-outline-into-a-first-draft/) once it is in.
